AI for Students, in plain words

Free AI tools for students help you study from your own notes, write faster and understand big projects, without paying for a subscription. The best ones show where each answer came from, so you learn the material instead of copying it. Every tool below is free and open source.

What free AI tools do for students

A study buddy that reads your notes. DeepTutor answers questions, makes quizzes and builds study plans from your own files, notes or a YouTube link. It has modes like Quiz, Solve and Mastery Path. You connect your own AI provider, like OpenAI, per the DeepTutor README.

Chat with your readings. Open Notebook is a free NotebookLM alternative you host yourself. Its answers come with citations, and it can turn your notes into a podcast with 1 to 4 speakers. With free local models through Ollama, you pay nothing for the AI.

Notes you own. Tolaria is a free offline notes app for Mac, Windows and Linux. Each note is a plain Markdown file, and every change is saved, so you can roll back mistakes. There's no account and no cloud to trust.

Talk instead of type. Handy turns your voice into text in any app, right on your own computer. It helps with first drafts of essays. You download a speech model once before it works.

Office files with AI. OfficeCLI lets an AI helper create, read and edit real Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, with no Office install needed, per its GitHub page.

For computer science students. Understand Anything turns a codebase into a clickable map with plain-English notes and guided tours. Matt Pocock's skills make AI coding helpers ask better questions and test their work.

Study smart with AI

Use AI to quiz yourself and explain hard parts, then check the answer in your own course material. Follow your school's rules on AI use for homework and exams. Some tools need your own AI account, and online AI services can cost money, so check before you connect one. For teachers, see our free AI tools for education.

FAQ

What are the best free AI tools for students?

DeepTutor for studying from your notes, Open Notebook for chatting with readings, Tolaria for notes and Handy for voice typing. All four are free and open source.

Can I use AI to study for free?

Yes. The tools are free. Some need an AI provider, but Open Notebook works with free local models through Ollama.

Is it OK to use AI for homework?

It depends on your school. Check its rules first, and use AI to learn and check your work, not to copy answers.
