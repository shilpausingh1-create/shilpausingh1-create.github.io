May 2026 in AI: Anthropic takes the crown

Google rebuilt search around AI, Musk lost his OpenAI lawsuit, Anthropic became the most valuable AI startup, and AI cracked an 80-year-old math puzzle.

EveryoneMarketingMediaFinanceGovernment & LawBusinessEducation

May reshuffled the leaderboard. Anthropic passed OpenAI to become the most valuable AI startup, and even rented computer power from its old critic, Elon Musk. Google changed the search box billions of people use, a jury ended Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI, and an AI model solved a math puzzle that had stood since 1946.

1. Google remakes its search bar with AI

What happened: At its I/O conference, Google showed a new search bar built for longer, chat-style questions, running on its Gemini 3.5 Flash model. Users can create "agents" (AI helpers that keep working on their own) that track things like apartment listings or sneaker drops, and search can build custom visuals and mini apps on request. Google also added Spark, a Gemini mode that works in the background on tasks like watching your inbox or credit card statements. CNN called it what may be Google's biggest shift yet away from traditional search.

Why it matters: For 25 years, search meant a list of blue links, so if Google answers questions directly, fewer people click through to websites. That changes how businesses get found online and how publishers make money. Google says Gemini now has more than 900 million active users, and it plans to spend about $180 billion to $190 billion on AI this year.

Who it affects: Marketers, SEO specialists, website owners, news publishers, online shops, small businesses and everyday searchers.

Source: CNN

2. Elon Musk loses his lawsuit against OpenAI

What happened: A federal jury in Oakland found that Musk waited too long to sue OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, after less than two hours of deliberation. The judge adopted the verdict right away. Musk had claimed OpenAI "stole a charity" by turning into a for-profit company and wanted up to $180 billion returned. The court did not rule on whether his claims were true, only that they came too late under a three-year legal deadline.

Why it matters: The loss cleared a big legal cloud over OpenAI as it prepares to go public, though the judge did not decide whether OpenAI broke any promise. Musk called it a "calendar technicality" and said he would appeal. The claims against Microsoft were also dismissed.

Who it affects: Investors, nonprofit and charity lawyers, startup founders, OpenAI and Microsoft.

Source: CNBC

3. Anthropic overtakes OpenAI as most valuable AI startup

What happened: Anthropic raised $65 billion at a $965 billion value, passing OpenAI's $852 billion. That is almost triple its value from February. It also reported a $47 billion yearly revenue pace, up from $30 billion earlier in the year. The round was led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital.

Why it matters: Just a few years ago, Anthropic was a distant second. Its rise came mostly from businesses paying for Claude Code and work tools. It also sets up a race between the two biggest AI labs to go public, with both preparing paperwork behind the scenes.

Who it affects: Investors, retirement savers, business leaders, developers and companies choosing between Claude and ChatGPT.

Source: CNBC

4. Anthropic rents Musk's giant data center

What happened: Anthropic agreed to use all the computing power at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center in Memphis, Tennessee, built by xAI. SpaceX's later stock market filing showed Anthropic will pay $1.25 billion a month through May 2029 (Bloomberg Law put the total near $45 billion), though either side can exit with 90 days' notice. Musk, who had harshly criticized Anthropic, said after meeting its team that "no one set off my evil detector." Anthropic had admitted demand was straining its systems at peak hours.

Why it matters: AI companies are so short of computing power that rivals now rent from each other. The Memphis site has a history of complaints about air pollution from gas turbines. It shows the AI boom's real-world cost in power and local health, which matters for any town being offered a new data center.

Who it affects: Claude users, energy and utility companies, Memphis residents, local officials weighing data centers, and investors.

Source: CNBC (price: Bloomberg Law)

5. OpenAI AI cracks 80-year-old math problem

What happened: OpenAI said a new reasoning model disproved a geometry conjecture first posed by mathematician Paul Erdős in 1946. Outside mathematicians, including Noga Alon, Melanie Wood and Thomas Bloom, publicly backed the proof. OpenAI said the result came from a general-purpose model, not one built for math. Months earlier, the company had been mocked for overstating a similar claim.

Why it matters: This time, independent experts checked the work. If AI can hold together long, hard chains of reasoning, it could speed up research in medicine, biology, physics and engineering, OpenAI says. Bloom called it a way to "more fully explore the cathedral of mathematics."

Who it affects: Scientists, math teachers, students, university researchers, engineers and drug developers.

Source: TechCrunch

What to watch next

  • OpenAI's confidential stock market filing, which CNBC said was coming within days or weeks, with a listing possible as soon as September.
  • Anthropic's own IPO preparations, which CNBC said were underway behind the scenes with timing still open.
  • SpaceX's IPO, after it filed its prospectus with the SEC, which will be the first big test of investor appetite for AI stocks.
  • Musk's planned appeal to the 9th Circuit court, though the trial judge said there was "a substantial amount of evidence" behind the jury's finding.
