Ex-OpenAI engineer in bugging case held as flight risk

By the ITG Now newsroom · News · 19 Oct 2026, 19:51 IST · Updated 20 Oct 2026

Share

Corrected 20 Oct: no opened source says prosecutors claim Saudi backing. Filings cite a one-way trip to Saudi Arabia as flight risk; Saudi ties come from his podcast comments and a venture role at a Riyadh firm. Headline and source fixed. All corrections

A former OpenAI engineer is charged with planting a hidden listening device in an executive meeting room at the company's San Francisco office. Prosecutors also pointed to a one-way trip to Saudi Arabia, and reports describe his Saudi business ties. The charges are claims by prosecutors, not a court finding.

What happened

The US Justice Department announced the case on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, according to Al Jazeera. The engineer is a 35-year-old Saudi national. He is charged with using or trying to use a device to intercept spoken conversations. That carries up to five years in prison and a $250,000 fine.

Prosecutors say a recorder wrapped in toilet paper was found on September 30 at a TV in an executive conference room. The Register says it was plugged into the TV's USB port, and the Boston Globe says it was behind the TV. They say it was put in around September 28. Security staff later reportedly found a second device in the engineer's bag, Al Jazeera reported.

The FBI arrested him on October 9, according to Al Jazeera. OpenAI says it fired him.

Key facts

  • The charge: Trying to intercept oral communications. It was filed as a complaint in the federal court for Northern California, per The Boston Globe and The Register.
  • The room: Only executives and their assistants could book it, the Boston Globe reported. It was used for leadership meetings and briefings with government officials.
  • The data: OpenAI could not retrieve any data after making a forensic image of the device, The Register reported. The memory card may have been encrypted. The sources we read do not say if it recorded anything.
  • Travel: Prosecutors say the engineer, his wife and a relative had one-way tickets to Saudi Arabia for October 12, per Al Jazeera.
  • OpenAI's statement: The company said it has no sign he improperly accessed or took model weights, source code or customer data, The Register reported.
  • Employment: He had worked at OpenAI since July 2025, reports The Register.

Background

The Register says prosecutors point to AI chatbot use. Between about August 17 and September 16, the engineer allegedly asked a work AI tool about building addresses, meeting rooms and calendars. Later, an account linked to his wife allegedly asked how to build a continuous audio recorder, per The Boston Globe.

On the Saudi link, the sources differ in how strong it is. The Boston Globe's account of the filings mentions Saudi Arabia only for the one-way tickets. The advisory work comes from his own podcast interview. In an Arabic podcast, the engineer said he and other Saudi tech workers formed a group that advised Saudi groups, including the national social insurance agency. A Riyadh venture firm also lists him as a venture partner.

His lawyer said he is presumed innocent and will fight the charges and his detention, per Al Jazeera. The lawyer also said he is not charged with stealing trade secrets or acting as a foreign agent, according to the Boston Globe.

What it means for you

Our take: AI labs hold work that rivals and governments want to know about. Even a small recorder in a meeting room can matter if the talks are secret.

The FBI's director said protecting this work is one of the FBI's highest counterintelligence priorities, The Register reported.

Our take: treat the "Saudi backing" idea with care. A person's country or work ties are not proof that a government sent him. The sources we read do not show that.

What happens next

The defense says it will contest the charges and detention, per Al Jazeera. The sources we read give no trial date and no report of a plea.

FAQ

What is the former OpenAI engineer charged with?

He is charged with using or trying to use a device to intercept spoken communications. The maximum penalty is five years in prison and a $250,000 fine, per Al Jazeera.

Did OpenAI lose data or secrets?

OpenAI says it has no sign that he took model weights, source code or customer data, per The Register. OpenAI could not retrieve any data from the device after making a forensic image, The Register reported.

Do prosecutors say Saudi Arabia was behind it?

The Boston Globe's account of the filings mentions Saudi Arabia only for the one-way tickets. Prosecutors pointed to a one-way trip to Saudi Arabia as a reason he might flee, per Al Jazeera.
