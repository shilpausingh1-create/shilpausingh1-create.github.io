Meta pulls web-scraping line from its business terms

By the ITG Now newsroom · News · 17 Oct 2026, 20:34 IST · Updated 21 Oct 2026

Share

Meta has removed a line from its new business terms after publishers worried it could let Meta use their websites to train AI. Meta says nothing about how it crawls the web has changed. The terms cover any business use of Meta products, such as managing a Facebook Page.

What happened

Meta had updated its commercial terms, the rules for businesses that use its products. The wording said Meta could "use any content from" a business account's domains, web pages or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research and train its products, TechCrunch reports.

Legal experts quoted by Nieman Lab called this a "remarkable provision," per TechCrunch. The Verge reported that the provision has now been removed, TechCrunch says. No exact day is given.

Meta said the update did not reflect a change in how it crawls the web. A Meta spokesperson said the crawling terms it merged applied only to its ads and business products.

Key facts

  • Removed wording: a line letting Meta use content from a business account's domains, pages or URLs for products and AI training, per TechCrunch.
  • Reach: Search Engine Journal says the clause covered any business use of Meta products, such as managing a Facebook Page, not only buying ads.
  • Start date: the terms were due to take effect on 30 October, according to Search Engine Journal.
  • Test credentials: the original wording also let Meta create "test credentials" to reach pages. Meta declined to say if that could get past paywalls, per Search Engine Journal.
  • Meta's position: the update is about pulling accurate product information for ad and content recommendations, not scraping news sites, per Search Engine Journal. TechCrunch quotes Meta saying it does not change how Meta uses web crawlers.
  • Robots.txt: Meta says site owners can still use this file to control access, but neither updated document mentions it, per Search Engine Journal.

Background

Earlier Meta ad terms already had a crawling clause. Search Engine Journal says that older clause covered advertisers' domains. The new Commercial Terms stretched it to every business using Meta products, including those that buy no ads.

AI training and copyright are still fought over. TechCrunch notes that Meta won a high-profile copyright case over AI training in 2025, but other cases against AI companies, Meta among them, are still going.

Meta also runs different crawlers. Search Engine Journal says one handles AI training and product improvement, and another supports Meta AI search. Site owners could block one and allow the other.

What it means for you

This affects any business that uses Meta products, including a small shop that only runs a Facebook Page and buys no ads. Search Engine Journal says continued business use counts as agreeing to the terms, and that a business that disagrees can stop using Meta for business.

The line that worried publishers is gone, and Meta says its web crawling is unchanged. Search Engine Journal said the terms were set to start on 30 October and that neither document mentioned robots.txt. No source says if the removal changed either point. Search Engine Journal also says two Meta tools may skip robots.txt rules in some cases, such as user-requested fetches.

What to watch: check your robots.txt rules for each Meta crawler. Our take: the removal calms things down, but the question is not settled.

What happens next

The sources do not give new steps. Search Engine Journal says the updated terms were set to start on 30 October.

Our take: watch whether Meta publishes plainer crawling rules, and whether publishers ask for them in writing.

FAQ

Did Meta say it will scrape my website for AI?

Meta says the update did not change how it crawls the web and is not about scraping news sites, per Search Engine Journal.

What did Meta remove?

It removed wording that let it use any content from a business account's domains, pages or URLs to train its products, per TechCrunch.

Can I still block Meta's crawlers?

Meta says site owners can use a robots.txt file, per Search Engine Journal. But neither updated terms document mentions it, and two Meta tools may skip robots.txt in some cases, such as user-requested fetches. Check your settings.
