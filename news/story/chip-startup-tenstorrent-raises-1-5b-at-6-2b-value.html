Chip startup Tenstorrent raises $1.5B at $6.2B value

By the ITG Now newsroom · News · 19 Oct 2026, 17:10 IST

Share

Tenstorrent, a company that designs computer chips for artificial intelligence, has raised $1.5 billion in new money. That values the company at $6.2 billion, more than double what it was worth in late 2024. The money will go toward scaling its chips for open-weight AI models and video generation.

What happened

TechCrunch reported the raise on October 19, 2026. The round was led by two investors: Trace Capital, a new firm started by a co-founder of Quiet Capital, and Innovation X.

Other backers include Cadence Ventures, Eclipse, Fidelity, SanDisk and T. Rowe Price, TechCrunch reported.

The company will use the cash to scale up chips built for open-weight AI models and for video generation. TechCrunch cited the company's chief executive telling Bloomberg that showing its Galaxy servers could run DeepSeek models helped it decide to stay independent and raise money.

Key facts

  • Amount raised: $1.5 billion, reported by TechCrunch on October 19, 2026.
  • Value of the company: $6.2 billion, per TechCrunch. That is up from $2.6 billion in late 2024.
  • What the chips do: They focus on inference, which means running an AI model after it has been trained. TechCrunch says they are tailored for open-weight models and video generation.
  • Lead investors: Trace Capital and Innovation X.
  • Stayed independent: Bloomberg reported on May 18, 2026, as cited by TechCrunch, that Intel and Qualcomm were interested in buying Tenstorrent at values up to $5 billion. The company chose to stay on its own.

Background

Tenstorrent was founded in 2016 and is based in Santa Clara, California, according to Crunchbase News. Its stated goal, per Crunchbase News, is to rival the biggest chip company in AI chips.

In late 2024, it announced a Series D round of more than $693 million. Crunchbase News noted that Bloomberg reported about $700 million in total, with a value of roughly $2.6 billion after the deal. That is the "late 2024" figure that the new round more than doubles.

Crunchbase News also said the company planned to spend that earlier money on open-source AI software, hiring developers, and building AI systems and cloud services.

The market around it is hot. TechCrunch notes that Etched, another AI chip maker, was fielding investment offers at values of $40 billion or more earlier in October 2026.

What it means for you

Open-weight models are AI models whose inner settings are shared publicly, so anyone can run them. Inference is the work of answering your questions with a model.

Our take: if more companies build good chips for open models, AI tools could get cheaper to run over time. That is a hope, not a promise. Nothing in the reports says what Tenstorrent will charge or when new chips arrive.

Our take: one company is not going to knock off the market leader quickly. Still, a $1.5 billion raise shows investors want other choices besides one dominant chip supplier.

What happens next

The sources we read do not give dates for new chips or servers. TechCrunch only says the money will go toward scaling chips for open-weight models and video generation.

Our take: watch for product launches and customer announcements. Those will show whether the money turns into chips people can buy.
