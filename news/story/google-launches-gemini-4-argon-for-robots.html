Google launches Gemini 4 Argon for robots

By the ITG Now newsroom · News · 25 Oct 2026, 04:46 IST

Share

Google has released Gemini 4 Argon, an AI model that it says understands the physical world well enough to help plan and train robots. Google announced it on 13 October 2026. Google also shows one test where practice data made by the model helped a robot do better.

What happened

Google's developer blog calls Argon a general-purpose physical reasoning model. It says the model is a step change in physical understanding, writing code and reading what it sees.

Google lists four jobs for it: controlling robots, building simulated copies of real places, physical understanding, and research and self-improvement. On a test of embodied reasoning called ERQA, Google says Argon scored 87%.

In one demo, Argon started from a single photo of a tabletop. It built a simulated copy of the scene and made more than 4,500 practice runs with small random changes. A robot trained on that data went from a 56% real-world success rate to 75%. Its task time dropped from 35 seconds to 24 seconds.

Key facts

  • Google says Argon scored 87% on ERQA, a test of embodied reasoning. eesel.ai found no independent verification of that result.
  • Training on more than 4,500 simulated runs raised a physical robot's success rate from 56% to 75%, Google reports.
  • The model is in the Gemini API on the paid tier under the name gemini-4-argon, with no free tier, according to eesel.ai's review.
  • Google says people with a Google AI Ultra plan can also use it in Google AI Studio and Google Antigravity.
  • eesel.ai lists the price as $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens until 30 November 2026, then $4 and $20 from 1 December.
  • Argon gives text output only. It does not support live streaming, so real-time robot control runs on a different Google model.

Background

Google's pitch is that Argon can build a practice world from a single photo.

Other demos in the coverage show the same idea. Interesting Engineering reports that a Google DeepMind post says Argon taught a humanoid robot from Apptronik to sink seven cornhole throws in a row after watching a video. It also says a Boston Dynamics Spot robot practiced opening a foot-pedal trash bin in simulation before trying it for real.

Argon is not only about robots. The same report says Google claims it cut the "spacetime cost" of one quantum subroutine (an arcsin routine) by more than 100 times compared with a published method. It also says that in an unattended 11-hour software run, Argon suggested six speed-ups and two were accepted after human review. The report says the gain applies to that one subroutine, not to quantum computing as a whole.

What it means for you

Argon is on the paid Gemini API tier, so robot builders, researchers and app developers who already pay for that API can try it. It can write code and make practice worlds from a photo.

eesel.ai notes that it found no independent verification of the benchmark score, and no robot-specific safety guidance.

Our take: the 56% to 75% jump is the number to watch, because Google measured it on physical robot tasks. Until others repeat it, treat it as Google's claim.

What happens next

Our take: watch for two things. First, whether outside labs repeat the 87% score and the robot results. Second, what happens to the price on 1 December, when eesel.ai says it is due to double.

FAQ

What is Gemini 4 Argon?

It is a Google AI model for physical reasoning. Google says it helps with robot control, simulation, physical understanding and research.

Can I use Gemini 4 Argon for free?

No. eesel.ai says it is only on the paid tier of the Gemini API. Google AI Ultra subscribers can also use it in Google AI Studio.

Does Argon control a robot in real time?

Not by itself. eesel.ai says it does not support live streaming, so real-time control uses a different Google model.
