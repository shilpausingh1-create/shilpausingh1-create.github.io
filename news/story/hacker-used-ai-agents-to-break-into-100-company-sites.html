Reuters: hacker used AI agents against 100+ company sites

Reuters
Share

Corrected 20 Oct: Deere and Cummins were not confirmed breached (Cummins is investigating a vendor's site; Deere saw no sign sensitive data was accessed). Claude Code use is 'possible', per NorForge. Researcher named by role only. All corrections

A researcher says one Chinese-speaking hacker used AI agents to try to break into more than 1,200 websites and got into more than 100 company sites. The cost was about $5,000 in AI usage. That works out to roughly $50 for each break-in.

What happened

Reuters reported the findings on October 19, 2026. A summary on wpnews says a NorForge researcher found the activity on an open-source AI platform called Cairn.

Cairn is an open-source tool that coordinates AI agents. The agents hunt for weak spots and try to break in. The hacker gave them short orders, under 100 Chinese characters, per wpnews. Gizmodo says some prompts were as simple as "fetch credit cards."

The campaign appeared to use DeepSeek and Anthropic's Claude Code, wpnews reported. NorForge's own write-up is more careful. It says one worker was labeled a Claude Code harness using a DeepSeek model, but calls that possible, not confirmed.

Key facts

  • Sites targeted: More than 1,200, as of late in the week before the report, per wpnews.
  • Companies breached: More than 100, per the Reuters report.
  • Cost: About $5,000 in AI tokens, or roughly $50 per successful hack. That is the researcher's estimate, per wpnews.
  • Speed: Successful break-ins typically took about 23 hours, per wpnews.
  • Start date: The campaign began September 25, per wpnews.
  • Success rate: About 10 percent, per Gizmodo.

Background

Reuters named Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins as victims, per wpnews. The replies were mixed: - Deere said it took fixes and had no sign sensitive data was accessed. - SharkNinja said only a standalone product-support site was hit and the threat was contained. - Cummins said it was investigating a possible attack on a site run by an outside vendor.

Other victims did not respond, per wpnews. Two smaller businesses also said they were hacked, per wpnews.

The researcher found about two dozen Cairn installations used for harm, starting around October 2, per wpnews. NorForge says some of the panels appeared completely unprotected. It says quick Shodan and Censys searches reveal hosts running AI tools, and some were left open. NorForge describes clusters of attackers and does not say they are the same actor as the Reuters hacker.

Gizmodo quotes the author of Cairn saying the tool was not designed for hacking attacks.

What it means for you

Gizmodo says the campaign targeted online shops in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan. NorForge says some hackers aimed to steal payment data.

Our take: the big shift is cost. One person with little effort and a few thousand dollars reached over 100 companies. Small shops will face more probing than before.

Our take: if you run a small online shop, check that your admin pages and file upload tools are locked down.

What happens next

The sources we read list no legal action or new fixes. NorForge says its research should inform defenses and that it has been working with Reuters to notify victims.

Our take: watch for other researchers reporting similar cases.

FAQ

Which AI tools did the hacker use?

The agents ran on Cairn. Reuters, via wpnews, said the campaign appeared to use DeepSeek and Claude Code. NorForge calls the Claude Code link possible, not confirmed.

How much did the hacking cost?

About $5,000 in AI usage, or roughly $50 per successful break-in, according to the researcher's estimate in the wpnews summary of the Reuters report.

Which companies were named?

Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, per wpnews. Each gave a different answer about the effect.
