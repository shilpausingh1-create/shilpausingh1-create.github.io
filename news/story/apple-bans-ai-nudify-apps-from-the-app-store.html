Apple spells out its ban on AI nudify apps

Corrected 19 Oct: we fixed details of this story after checking the source again.

Apple has updated its App Store rules to go after apps that use AI to make fake nude images of real people without their consent. It also tightened rules on apps that hide features from Apple's reviewers. The update was posted on October 16, 2026.

What happened

Apple announced changes to its App Review Guidelines on October 16. Apple says the changes build on rules that already existed against sexual content and misrepresentation.

Three parts changed. First, Guideline 1.2, which covers apps where users post content, now names nonconsensual intimate imagery, including images made with AI tools. Apple says apps must work to prevent and remove it. Developers who lack safeguards, or who do not fix violations after notice, risk breaking the Developer Code of Conduct and being removed.

Second, Guideline 2.5.19 covers "bait-and-switch" apps. Apple describes these as apps that misrepresent their purpose during review and reveal harmful features only after approval. Apps caught doing this can be removed from the App Store and from the Apple Developer Program.

Third, the Section 5 language on human trafficking and child exploitation is stronger. Such apps will be removed and blocked from alternative distribution.

Key facts

  • Apple's post is dated October 16, 2026, and gives no separate effective date, per Apple.
  • Apple says it prevented over 150,000 apps with safety issues from reaching customers last year.
  • Apple also says it ended over 193,000 developer accounts for fraud and abuse last year.
  • 9to5Mac says the new Section 2.5.19 bans hidden, dormant or undocumented features, and names code obfuscation as grounds for removal.
  • Apple did not name any specific apps in its post.

Background

Apple says pornographic material and "nudification" apps were already prohibited. 9to5Mac lays out the pressure that came first:

  • In January 2026, a watchdog group called the Tech Transparency Project found dozens of AI "nudify" apps on the App Store.
  • Also in January, lawmakers and advocacy groups urged Apple to remove X and Grok over sexualized AI images, including of women and minors.
  • In April, a follow-up report found that App Store search suggestions and ads steered users to nudify apps. 9to5Mac says Apple confirmed that it had privately warned xAI that Grok could be removed unless the issue was fixed.
  • In July, San Francisco officials ordered Apple to remove eight apps able to make nonconsensual intimate images.
  • In July, a 9to5Mac investigation found dozens of gambling apps disguised as simple games and tools, some showing betting pages only when opened from Brazil.

What it means for you

If you use an iPhone or iPad, or your kids do, the store should have fewer apps that make fake nude images or turn into something else after approval. Apple says it will keep updating its tools to catch this behavior faster.

Our take: the headline "ban" is partly a clearer rulebook. Apple says these apps were already not allowed, and the update spells it out and adds penalties. The real test is whether Apple finds and removes the apps that slip through. Past reports show that some did.

Our take: if you are a parent, a rule change does not replace checking which apps are on a child's phone.

What happens next

Apple says it will keep updating its tools to detect this behavior faster. Our take: watch for removals of specific apps and for any new reports from watchdog groups testing the store.

FAQ

What exactly did Apple change?

It updated Guideline 1.2 to name AI-made nonconsensual intimate images, added Guideline 2.5.19 against bait-and-switch apps, and strengthened Section 5 on child exploitation, per Apple.

What is a bait-and-switch app?

Apple says it is an app that hides its true purpose during review, then shows harmful features after it is approved.

Were nudify apps allowed before?

Apple says they were already prohibited under existing rules. 9to5Mac reports that some still reached the store in 2026.
