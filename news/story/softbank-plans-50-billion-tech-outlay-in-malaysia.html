SoftBank plans $50 billion tech outlay in Malaysia

By the ITG Now newsroom · News · 25 Oct 2026, 13:12 IST · Updated 26 Oct 2026

Share

Malaysia's prime minister says the founder of SoftBank Group has picked places in the country for $50 billion of technology investment, The Business Times reported from Bloomberg. The money would go to AI, new technology and data centres. SoftBank had not commented when the report ran.

What happened

Malaysia's prime minister said on Saturday, Oct 24, in a speech in Melaka, that he held a video call with SoftBank's founder on Tuesday, according to The Business Times. The founder told him he had chosen Sarawak, including the city of Kuching, and peninsular Malaysia for the investment.

The prime minister described it as among the largest investments in the region, worth $50 billion. He named three areas: artificial intelligence, new technology and data centres. A data centre is a large building full of computers that run websites and AI tools.

SoftBank representatives did not immediately respond to a request for comment outside normal business hours, the report says. So the plan so far comes from the prime minister's account of the call.

Key facts

  • The planned spending is $50 billion in Malaysia, per the prime minister's speech on Oct 24.
  • The founder picked Sarawak (including Kuching) and peninsular Malaysia, the prime minister said.
  • The areas are AI, new technology and data centres, per the same speech.
  • Malaysia drew 385.7 billion ringgit ($94 billion) of data-centre investment from 2021 through the first half of 2026, according to the Malaysian Investment Development Authority.
  • Earlier this month, SoftBank said it signed framework agreements with partners to explore AI infrastructure in Sarawak.

Background

SoftBank is already building big AI projects elsewhere. The report says it is working on a $500 billion data centre project in Ohio, one of the largest in the world, and a project in France that could cost as much as 75 billion euros ($84 billion). In Japan, its telecom unit is building data centres in Hokkaido and in Sakai, Osaka.

Malaysia has become a major home for data centres. The report says Amazon, Microsoft and Google's parent company Alphabet have all invested there. The government now wants more value from the boom. It is pushing data-centre operators to use local suppliers, and to use the buildings to help AI adoption, home-grown ideas and higher-skilled jobs. Malaysia has set a goal of becoming an "AI nation" by 2030.

What it means for you

This news affects Malaysian workers, local suppliers and anyone who follows where AI computing power is being built. The prime minister said the investment would create new and better job opportunities, especially for younger workers. He also said Malaysia needs stronger skills in AI, digital work and chips to move from back-end tasks into front-end ones. Nothing in the report says this changes any product you use today. Our take: the plan is a promise from one side of a video call, so treat the $50 billion as a stated goal until SoftBank gives its own details and dates. If you work in tech in South-east Asia, watch for hiring and supplier calls tied to Sarawak.

What happens next

The report does not give a timeline, a price split or the names of partners for the Malaysia plan. SoftBank had not yet responded. Our take: watch for a SoftBank statement and for any signed agreement that turns the Sarawak framework deals into named projects.
