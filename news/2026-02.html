February 2026 in AI: Mega deals, market jitters, a Pentagon fight

Musk merged SpaceX and xAI, Anthropic raised $30 billion, new office AI shook software stocks, and the Pentagon clashed with Anthropic.

EveryoneFinanceJobs & CareersBusinessGovernment & LawDevelopers

February was about money and power, as AI companies raised and merged at sizes never seen before, and Wall Street got nervous that AI could replace whole categories of software. At the same time, a fight broke out over who decides how AI is used in war. Together, these stories show AI turning into a matter of national power, not just a tech product.

1. SpaceX buys xAI in biggest merger ever

What happened: Elon Musk's rocket company SpaceX acquired his AI company xAI, which makes the Grok chatbot. The deal valued the combined company at $1.25 trillion: SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion. It is the largest merger of all time. Musk said the main reason is to build "orbital data centers," meaning computer centers in space, and to give xAI more money to compete.

Why it matters: xAI was burning through cash to keep up with OpenAI and Anthropic, and SpaceX gives it deep pockets. It also set up a giant stock market listing, since SpaceX was already planning to go public later in the year. One person now controls rockets, the Starlink satellite internet service, a social network and a major AI lab, which raises new questions for regulators and investors alike.

Who it affects: Investors, the space and telecom industries, X users, regulators.

Source: CNBC

2. Anthropic raises $30 billion at $380 billion value

What happened: Anthropic, the maker of Claude, closed a $30 billion funding round at a $380 billion value. That is more than double its value from September, when it was worth $183 billion. The company said its yearly revenue pace hit $14 billion, and its coding tool Claude Code alone reached a $2.5 billion pace. About 80% of its business comes from companies, not consumers.

Why it matters: Building AI costs a fortune in computer chips, so the top labs keep raising record sums. This was the second-largest private tech funding round ever, behind only OpenAI. It shows investors believe businesses will pay heavily for AI that does real work, like writing code, analyzing data and drafting documents.

Who it affects: Investors, business leaders, software developers, startups.

Source: CNBC

3. AI fears trigger big software stock selloff

What happened: Anthropic added new plugins to Claude Cowork, an "AI colleague" that can read files, organize folders and draft documents, aimed at fields like sales, finance, marketing and legal. Investors panicked. Thomson Reuters fell 15.83% in one day, its biggest drop on record. LegalZoom sank nearly 20%, RELX (owner of LexisNexis) fell 14%, and a software industry fund had its worst day since April.

Why it matters: Many companies pay monthly for software that helps with research, data and legal work. If AI can do those jobs directly, they may need fewer subscriptions. Some analysts called the selloff overblown and "sentiment driven," pointing out that a similar panic over DeepSeek in 2025 faded within a year, but the fear itself shows how seriously markets take AI.

Who it affects: Investors, lawyers, legal and financial data firms, software companies, office workers.

Source: CNN

4. Anthropic launches Claude Opus 4.6

What happened: Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6, its top model, saying it is better at coding, longer tasks, research and financial analysis. TechCrunch reported it can take in 1 million "tokens" of context (roughly a very long stack of documents at once) and added "agent teams," where several AI helpers split up a job and work on it together. It took the top spot on a test of financial analyst tasks, and it is available on claude.ai and all major cloud platforms.

Why it matters: Anthropic's product head called this the shift from "vibe coding" to "vibe working." In plain terms, AI is going from answering small questions to taking on real chunks of work. That is exactly what worried software investors the same week, and it is why people in finance, research and consulting are watching these releases closely.

Who it affects: Office workers, financial analysts, researchers, developers.

Source: CNBC (second report: TechCrunch)

5. Pentagon clashes with Anthropic over AI limits

What happened: Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth gave Anthropic a deadline to drop limits on how the military can use Claude, or lose its $200 million contract. Anthropic refused to budge on two red lines: AI-controlled weapons and mass surveillance of Americans. The Pentagon threatened to label Anthropic a "supply chain risk," a tag usually reserved for companies tied to foreign enemies like Russia or China. The Pentagon said it has "always followed the law" and that legality is its responsibility as the user.

Why it matters: This is a test of whether an AI company can say no to its own government. The label would stop military contractors from using Claude in their defense work, which could hurt Anthropic with many large companies. A former Justice Department official told CNN the threat looked "more punitive" than legitimate.

Who it affects: Government agencies, defense contractors, lawyers, AI companies.

Source: CNN

What to watch next

  • SpaceX's planned stock market listing, which the Financial Times reported is aimed for mid-June and could raise up to $50 billion.
  • Whether the Pentagon follows through on the supply chain risk label.
  • OpenAI's own giant funding round, reported to be heading toward about $100 billion.
  • Whether software stocks recover as companies start to see measurable results from AI, which analysts at Aurelion Research expect will calm the mood.
