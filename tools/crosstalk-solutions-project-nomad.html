Project NOMAD: Wikipedia, maps and AI that work with no internet

A free offline knowledge box that puts Wikipedia, medical guides, Khan Academy courses, maps and a local AI on your own computer.

EveryoneEducationHealthcare

stars: 40K · license: Apache-2.0 · platforms: Linux (Ubuntu/Debian), Windows via WSL2

The problem it solves

We look up almost everything online. A recipe. A rash. A route. A math lesson for a kid.

Then the internet goes down. Maybe a storm hit. Maybe you live far from town. Maybe you are on a boat, at a cabin, or in a classroom with weak Wi-Fi. Suddenly all that knowledge is gone.

Project NOMAD fixes that. It keeps a big library of useful knowledge on your own machine, so it works with no internet at all.

What it is

Project NOMAD is a free, offline "knowledge server." That is a computer that stores information and serves it to you through a web browser.

You install it once while you still have internet. It downloads the tools and content you pick. After that, it runs on its own. The makers say it has zero built-in tracking.

Under the hood, it bundles several well-known free projects. You do not have to set them up one by one. NOMAD has a control panel, called the Command Center, that installs, sets up and updates everything for you.

Who it's for

  • Families who want to be ready for storms or power and internet outages.
  • People in rural areas, on boats, in RVs, or off the grid.
  • Teachers and parents who want Khan Academy lessons that work offline.
  • Clinics, camps or aid workers who need medical references without a signal.
  • Hobbyists who like building a home server.

You will need some comfort with a computer. Setup happens by typing commands, not by clicking an installer.

What you can do with it

From the README, NOMAD includes:

  • Offline library: Wikipedia, medical references, survival guides and ebooks, through a tool called Kiwix.
  • Learning platform: Khan Academy courses with progress tracking and support for several users, through Kolibri.
  • Offline maps: download maps of your region to view and search with no connection.
  • AI chat: a local AI assistant that runs on your machine. You can upload your own documents and ask questions about them.
  • Notes: a simple note-taking app that stays on your device.
  • Data tools: encryption, encoding and analysis tools through CyberChef.
  • App catalog ("Supply Depot"): one-click extra apps like PDF tools, a file browser, an e-book library and a password manager.
  • Hardware score: test your machine and compare it on a community leaderboard.
  • Setup wizard: a guided first run with ready-made content bundles.
  • Automatic updates: optional, on a schedule you choose.

You open all of it in a normal web browser. Other devices on your home network can use it too.

How to start, step by step

What you need first: a computer running Ubuntu or Debian (two free versions of Linux). The makers recommend Ubuntu 26.04 LTS. Windows users can try a community-supported path using WSL2 (a way to run Linux inside Windows). You also need internet during setup, and admin rights on the machine.

  1. Open the Terminal on your Ubuntu or Debian computer.
  2. Paste the quick install command from the README and press Enter:
sudo apt-get update && \
sudo apt-get install -y curl && \
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Crosstalk-Solutions/project-nomad/refs/heads/main/install/install_nomad.sh \
  -o install_nomad.sh && \
sudo bash install_nomad.sh
  1. When it finishes, open a web browser and go to http://localhost:8080.
  2. Follow the setup wizard and pick the content you want, like Wikipedia, maps or courses.
  3. Let the downloads finish while you still have internet. After that, you are ready to go offline.

The project website has a full step-by-step guide, including how to install Ubuntu itself.

Good to know

  • Space and power: NOMAD itself needs about 5 GB of disk and under 1 GB of memory. The content you add is what fills the drive.
  • The AI part needs a strong computer. Adding the AI assistant brings the install to about 25 GB. For good results, the makers suggest 32 GB of memory, a graphics card like an NVIDIA RTX 3060, and at least 250 GB of free space.
  • No passwords built in. Anyone who can reach it on your network can use it. The makers say not to put it on the open internet unless you really know what you are doing.
  • Privacy: it only goes online during install, or when you choose to download more content.
  • Cost: the software is free. Your only cost is the computer and storage you run it on.
  • License (Apache-2.0): free to use, change and even use in a business. Just keep the license notice.

The bottom line

Project NOMAD turns a spare computer into an offline library, classroom, map and AI helper. Setup takes some typing, but after that it keeps working when the internet does not. It is a smart backup for any home, school or remote team.
