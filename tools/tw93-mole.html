Mole: a free way to deep-clean your Mac

Mole is a free, open-source tool that cleans junk, fully uninstalls apps, and shows what is filling up your Mac.

EveryoneDevelopers

stars: 70K · license: GPL-3.0 · platforms: Mac (Windows version in progress)

The problem it solves

Your Mac used to feel fast. Now the disk is nearly full and you get "storage almost full" pop-ups.

A lot of that space is junk you never see. Cache files (saved copies apps keep to load faster). Old logs. Leftovers from apps you dragged to the Trash years ago. Big installer files sitting in your Downloads folder.

Paid cleaner apps can fix this, but many want a yearly fee. Mole does much of the same work for free.

What it is

Mole is a free, open-source Mac cleaning toolkit. Open source means the code is public for anyone to read and check.

Its maker describes it as CleanMyMac, AppCleaner, DaisyDisk, and iStat Menus style tools rolled into one. It can clean, uninstall apps, do upkeep, map your disk, and show live system health.

One important detail: Mole runs in Terminal, the Mac's built-in app where you type commands. There are no buttons or windows. You type a short command, then use your arrow keys to pick from simple menus. It sounds techy, but the main command is just two letters: mo.

There is also a separate app with buttons, called Mole for Mac, sold at mole.fit. That is a different product. This article covers the free Terminal version.

Who it's for

  • Mac users who are tired of "disk full" warnings.
  • Anyone who does not want to pay for a cleaner app.
  • Designers and video editors with huge apps and files to manage.
  • Developers, who get extra tools for clearing out old project files.
  • People who are okay typing a few short commands.

What you can do with it

  • Deep clean. mo clean removes caches, logs, temp files, and leftovers from apps you already deleted. It also empties the Trash by default.
  • Uninstall apps fully. mo uninstall removes an app plus the related files it left around your Mac. It keeps your personal settings by default, unless you choose to remove them.
  • Map your disk. mo analyze shows a bar chart of what takes up the most space. You can move items to the Trash after you confirm.
  • Check your Mac's health. mo status shows a live dashboard with CPU, memory, disk, network, and battery, plus an overall health score.
  • Do upkeep. mo optimize refreshes things like icon and preview caches and network settings.
  • Clear old installers. mo installer finds leftover DMG and PKG install files in places like Downloads and the Desktop.
  • Clean coding projects. mo purge finds big rebuildable folders that coding projects create.
  • Preview first. Add --dry-run to see what would happen without deleting anything.

How to start, step by step

You need a Mac running macOS 12 or newer. It works on both Intel and Apple Silicon Macs. The easiest install uses Homebrew, a free app installer for the Mac.

  1. Open Terminal. Press Command and Space, type "Terminal," and press Enter.
  2. Paste this line and press Enter:
brew install mole

No Homebrew? The README also offers a one-line install script you can paste instead.

  1. Type mo and press Enter. A menu appears.
  2. Use the arrow keys to pick clean, uninstall, or analyze.
  3. Want to be extra safe? Run mo clean --dry-run first. It shows what Mole would remove, without removing it.

Good to know

  • It is free. The Terminal tool costs nothing. The app with buttons, Mole for Mac, is a separate paid-for product from the same maker.
  • It can delete files. Clean, uninstall, purge, and installer can remove things for good. The README advises using --dry-run first.
  • It is careful by design. Mole checks paths, protects system files, and skips anything it cannot prove is safe. It asks for your admin password only when needed.
  • You can protect things. A "whitelist" (a list of items to never touch) lets you keep caches you want, or keep your Trash from being emptied.
  • It keeps a log. Mole records what it cleaned, and you can review it with mo history.
  • Watch out for fakes. The maker says Mole only comes from Homebrew, its GitHub page, and mole.fit. Other sites offering a "Mole-based" app are not connected to it.
  • Windows is early. A Windows version exists on a separate branch, but the main focus is the Mac.
  • License: GPL-3.0. You are free to use it and change it. But if you share a changed version, you must share its code under the same license too.

The bottom line

Mole packs a Mac cleaner, app remover, disk map, and health monitor into one free tool. It asks you to type a few commands, but the menus are simple and the dry-run option makes it safe to explore. If you are happy with Terminal, it can save you the cost of a paid cleaner.
