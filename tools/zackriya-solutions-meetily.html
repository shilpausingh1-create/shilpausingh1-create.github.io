Meetily: free AI meeting notes that stay on your computer

A free, private meeting assistant that transcribes and summarizes your calls on your own computer, even offline.

BusinessHealthcareEveryone

stars: 32K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux

The problem it solves

AI meeting note-takers are handy. They write down what everyone said and give you a neat summary. But most of them send your recordings to a company's servers. Some even join your call as a "bot" that everyone can see.

That is a real worry when you talk about private things. Client details. Patient cases. Legal strategy. Salary talks. Once a recording leaves your computer, you do not control where it goes or who sees it.

What it is

Meetily is a free, open-source AI meeting assistant. It captures your meetings, writes them out as text in real time, and creates summaries. The key part: it can do all of this on your own computer, without sending your data to the cloud.

It uses free AI speech models called Whisper and Parakeet to turn speech into text on your device. For summaries, the team recommends Ollama (a free app that runs AI models on your own computer).

Who it's for

  • Consultants and freelancers who discuss client work on calls.
  • Lawyers and legal teams handling sensitive cases.
  • Doctors, therapists and healthcare staff who need private notes.
  • Managers and HR teams running one-on-ones and reviews.
  • Journalists and researchers recording interviews.
  • Students who want transcripts of lectures or study calls.
  • Anyone who wants AI notes without another company hearing their talks.

What you can do with it

  • Live transcript. See the words appear as people talk.
  • Local transcription. Turn speech into text on your device with Whisper or Parakeet models.
  • AI summaries. Get a summary of each meeting. Ollama (local) is recommended. Claude, Groq, OpenRouter and OpenAI also work.
  • Use your own AI server. Connect any OpenAI-compatible service, handy for companies with their own AI setup.
  • Import old recordings. Bring in existing audio files to get transcripts. This is a beta (test) feature.
  • Re-transcribe. Run a recorded meeting again with a different model or language.
  • Capture both sides. Records your microphone and your computer's sound at the same time, with smart volume balancing.
  • Faster on good hardware. Uses your computer's graphics chip to speed things up where supported.
  • Works offline. The README says it works offline and supports multiple meeting platforms.

How to start, step by step

On a Mac:

First you need: a Mac with Apple silicon (the download is built for M-series chips).

  1. Go to the project's Releases page on GitHub.
  2. Download the Meetily .dmg file.
  3. Open it and drag Meetily into your Applications folder.
  4. Open Meetily from Applications.
  5. Start a meeting and watch the live transcript appear.

On Windows:

First you need: a 64-bit PC whose processor supports AVX2 (most PCs from recent years do).

  1. Download the latest x64-setup.exe from the Releases page.
  2. Run the installer.
  3. Open Meetily and start a meeting.

On Linux: there is no ready-made installer. You build it from the code using the project's Linux guide.

Good to know

  • Privacy depends on your AI choice. Transcription happens on your device. If you pick a cloud AI like Claude or OpenAI for summaries, your transcript text goes to that company. For full privacy, use Ollama.
  • Recording laws. Recording people without consent can be illegal in some places. Always tell others you are recording.
  • There is a paid PRO version. Meetily PRO is a separate product with higher accuracy, custom summary templates, PDF and Word exports, automatic meeting detection, and team features. Pricing is on their website. The free Community Edition stays free and open source, according to the README.
  • Speaker labels are PRO. Telling speakers apart automatically is listed for PRO, not the free version.
  • Linux takes effort. You need to build it yourself, which requires Rust and Node.js.
  • License. It uses MIT. In plain words: free to use, change and share, even at work, as long as you keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

Meetily gives you AI meeting notes without handing your private calls to a cloud service. Transcription runs on your own computer, and with a local AI model, summaries do too. If your meetings are sensitive, it is one of the easiest private options to try.
