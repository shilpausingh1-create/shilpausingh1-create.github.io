Open Design: a free AI studio for prototypes, decks and dashboards

Open Design is a free, open-source AI design studio that makes on-brand prototypes, slide decks, dashboards and images using the AI you already have.

DesignMarketingBusiness

stars: 101K · license: Apache-2.0 · platforms: Mac, Windows (Linux from source)

The problem it solves

You have an idea for a website, an app screen or a pitch deck. You want to see it before you spend real money building it. So you open a design tool. Then you hit a monthly fee. Or a learning curve. Or both.

And when you finally get something, it often does not look like your brand. The colors are off. The fonts are wrong. You end up fixing it by hand.

For founders, marketers and small teams, this slows everything down.

What it is

Open Design is a free, open-source AI design workspace. Open source means anyone can read and check the code. You start with a short brief, like "a landing page for my bakery." The AI then builds a working prototype (a clickable early version) that follows your design system (your brand's colors, fonts and style rules).

You can share the result with your team for feedback. Then the AI keeps refining it in the same project.

Here is the clever part. Open Design does not ship its own AI. It uses the AI coding tools you may already have on your computer, such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or GitHub Copilot. If you do not have one, you can paste in a key from an AI provider instead. This is often called "bring your own key."

Who it's for

  • Founders who want to test an idea before hiring a designer.
  • Marketers who need landing pages, campaign visuals and decks fast.
  • Product managers who want to show, not just describe, a new feature.
  • Designers who want a quick first draft to react to.
  • Small business owners who need a simple site mock-up or a pitch deck.
  • Developers who want designs they can hand straight to their coding AI.

What you can do with it

  • Prototypes for web, desktop and mobile. The README shows a dashboard and a three-screen mobile app as examples.
  • Live dashboards you can tweak in place, like a KPI wall (a screen of key numbers).
  • Slide decks with 15 deck templates and 36 themes. Decks export to HTML, PDF, PowerPoint (PPTX), ZIP or Markdown.
  • Images made from your conversation, with 93 ready-made prompt templates.
  • Motion graphics and video through a built-in tool called HyperFrames, exported as MP4.
  • Stay on brand. Every design reads your brand file first. The app ships with 151 design systems you can start from.
  • Refresh an existing product. Hand it your code and your brand file, and the AI can restyle your real screens to match.
  • Use 100+ skills and plugins (add-on abilities you can try with one click).
  • Use it without the app. You can also call Open Design from inside your AI coding tool.

How to start, step by step

What you need first: a Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) or a Windows PC. It helps to have an AI coding tool like Claude Code or Codex installed. If not, you will need an API key (a private password-like code) from an AI provider.

  1. Go to the Releases page on GitHub, or the project's website, open-design.ai.
  2. Download the desktop app for your system.
  3. Install it like any normal app.
  4. Open it. It looks for AI coding tools on your computer by itself.
  5. Type a short brief in the box. For example: "a landing page for my bakery, warm colors, online order button."
  6. Watch the live preview. Ask for changes in plain words until you like it.
  7. Share it with your team, or export it.

On Linux? There is no ready-made Linux app yet. You can run it from the source code, but that takes more technical setup.

Good to know

  • The app is free, but AI is not always free. Open Design uses your AI tool or key, so your normal AI costs apply.
  • There are paid options. The makers sell a plan called OpenDesign Go and a hosted service called OpenDesign Cloud. You do not need them to use the free app.
  • Privacy: Projects can run on your own computer. But any request to a cloud AI model or to OpenDesign Cloud is sent to that service.
  • Some data is shared. The README says product analytics are only on if you agree. Basic safety and reliability data (with private details removed) is always on.
  • Mac tip: Macs have a built-in command also called od. If you want to connect Open Design to your coding tool, the README suggests copying the setup text from the app's Settings, under MCP server, instead.
  • License: Apache-2.0. You can use it, change it and use it for work, for free. Just keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

Open Design turns a one-line idea into a real, on-brand prototype, deck or dashboard, without a design subscription. If you already pay for an AI coding tool, it puts that tool to work as your designer.

Get it: github.com/nexu-io/open-design
