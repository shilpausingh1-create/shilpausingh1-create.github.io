OmniRoute: one free hub to connect your AI tools to many models

A free hub that links your AI tools to many AI providers and switches to another when one runs out.

DevelopersBusinessStudents

stars: 77K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux

The problem it solves

You are in the middle of a project with an AI helper. Then it stops. "Usage limit reached. Try again in a few hours." Your flow is gone.

Many AI companies offer free tiers (a set amount you can use for free each month). But using them is a pain. Each one has its own account, its own key, its own limits, and its own setup. Switching between them by hand is slow and confusing.

What it is

OmniRoute is a free, open-source AI gateway. Think of it as a smart power strip for AI. You plug your AI tools into OmniRoute once. OmniRoute connects to many AI providers behind the scenes.

When one provider hits its limit, fails, or gets too pricey, OmniRoute can move your request to the next healthy option automatically. You keep working.

It runs on your own computer and gives you a dashboard in your browser. The project lists hundreds of providers and more than 1,200 AI models, with many providers offering free tiers.

Who it's for

  • Developers who use AI coding tools all day and keep hitting limits.
  • Students and hobbyists who want to stretch free AI tiers as far as they go.
  • Small teams and startups watching their AI bills.
  • Tinkerers who like trying different AI models side by side.

Be aware: this is a technical tool. It is aimed at people who use AI coding helpers and are fine using Terminal (the text window where you type commands). If you only use ChatGPT in a browser, you probably do not need it.

What you can do with it

  • One address for everything. Point your AI tools to one local address and reach many providers.
  • Auto-switching. Set the model to "auto" and OmniRoute picks from your connected providers, scoring them live.
  • Pick a goal. Choose options like coding quality, fastest, cheapest, or "only use the plan I already pay for."
  • Build your own chains. Line up models in order, with 19 routing styles, such as "use this one until it runs out, then the next."
  • Works with popular AI tools. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Cline, Aider, Continue, Goose, OpenCode and more.
  • Launch a tool through it with one command, like omniroute run claude.
  • See your free quota. A dashboard page shows the free tiers you can use.
  • Chat in Terminal. A built-in chat app lets you talk to models directly.
  • Run it your way. Install with npm, run in Docker, or build a desktop app.

How to start, step by step

First you need: Node.js (a free tool that runs many programs). The README lists version 22 or 24. You also need at least one account with an AI provider you can use.

  1. Open Terminal and install OmniRoute:
npm install -g omniroute
  1. Start it:
omniroute

Prefer a guided tour? Run omniroute setup for a first-run wizard.

  1. Open the dashboard at http://localhost:20128 in your browser.
  2. Go to Providers, pick a provider whose terms and quota suit you, and add its key or sign in.
  3. Copy your OmniRoute key from Dashboard, Endpoints.
  4. In your AI coding tool, set the base address to http://localhost:20128/v1, paste your OmniRoute key, and set the model to auto.

If npm shows warnings during install, the README says the common ones are harmless. Newer npm versions may ask you to allow install scripts. The README gives the exact command.

Good to know

  • Free tiers are not from OmniRoute. You bring your own provider accounts. Each provider sets its own limits and terms, and can change them any time.
  • Some free tiers need more. The README says free tiers may require signup, approval, or a payment method.
  • "Auto" needs a connection. A fresh install may have nothing to route to until you connect a provider.
  • Some providers are skipped. Providers marked as risky under their terms of service are left out of automatic routing by default.
  • Paid add-on exists. An optional service called Radar gives supporters a fresher free-model list. Everything else stays free.
  • Affiliate links. The README includes partner links that fund the project.
  • Privacy. OmniRoute runs on your machine, but your prompts still go to whichever AI provider answers them.
  • License. It uses MIT. In plain words: free to use, change and share, even in paid work, as long as you keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

OmniRoute is a smart switchboard for AI. It connects your coding tools to many providers and jumps to the next one when a limit hits. If you live inside AI coding tools and hate waiting for limits to reset, it is worth a look.
