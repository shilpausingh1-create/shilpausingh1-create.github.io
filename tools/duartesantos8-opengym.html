openGym: a free workout tracker where you own your data

A free workout and body-weight tracker you run yourself, with plans, guided sessions, 5,600+ exercises and no subscription or ads.

EveryoneHealthcare

stars: 12K · license: AGPL-3.0 · platforms: Your own server (Docker); use on phone or laptop, Android app

The problem it solves

Most workout apps follow the same pattern. The basics are free. The good stuff sits behind a subscription. Your training history lives on their servers. And if the company shuts down, years of logged workouts can vanish.

You do the hard work in the gym. Your data should belong to you.

openGym is a workout tracker you run yourself, so your history stays in a folder you control.

What it is

openGym is a free, open-source gym and body-weight tracker. You "self-host" it, which means you run it on your own computer or home server instead of a company's.

It still feels like a modern app. You can add it to your phone's home screen, sign in with a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID or fingerprint), use it offline, and sync between your phone and laptop.

The README promises no account on someone else's server, no subscription, no ads and no tracking.

There's also an online demo with example data, so you can try it before installing anything.

Who it's for

  • Gym-goers and lifters who log every set and want serious progress tracking.
  • Beginners who want a ready-made plan and guided workouts.
  • Personal trainers and coaches who like planning routines in detail.
  • Privacy-minded people who don't want health data on a company's server.
  • Home-lab hobbyists who like running their own apps.

What you can do with it

Planning - Build a routine for each weekday from over 5,600 exercises with animated demos. - Browse exercises by muscle on a body map, and filter by the equipment you own. - Start with four ready plans: Push/Pull/Legs, Upper/Lower, Full Body, or 5×5. - Use a rotation (A, B, C, A...) instead of fixed weekdays. - Add supersets, warm-up sets, drop sets, timed exercises like planks, and cardio.

Training - Guided sessions that pre-fill your weights from last time. - A rest timer between sets, which can flash the screen in loud gyms. - Personal records spotted as you go. - Plate math that works out which plates to load, based on the plates you own.

Progress - Progression rules that tell you when to add weight, and explain why. - A muscle map that shows where your training went, what's still recovering, and what you've been neglecting. - A body-weight chart against your goal, and progress photos with a before/after slider. - A year-long activity heatmap.

Your data - Import your history from FitNotes, Strong, Hevy and Apple Health weight exports. - Export everything as one file whenever you like. - Share or print a plan as a PDF. - 19 languages.

Optional extras (off by default) - An AI coach that drafts a week of routines and suggests changes. You approve every change. It uses your own AI account key.

How to start, step by step

You need Docker with Compose. Docker is a free app that runs other apps in a neat, self-contained box.

  1. Install Docker from docker.com.
  2. Open Terminal and paste these lines:
git clone https://github.com/DuarteSantos8/openGym
cd openGym
cp .env.example .env
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d
  1. Open the app. Go to http://localhost:8080 in your browser.
  2. Tap "Create profile" and you're in. The exercise animations are already included.
  3. Reach it from your phone (optional). Passkeys from a phone need a secure web address (HTTPS). The self-hosting guide walks through setting this up.

Just want a phone app? Android users can download the app file (APK) from the latest release. It runs on its own with no account and no server, and everything stays on the phone. It is not on the Play Store. iPhone users can add the web app to their home screen from Safari.

Good to know

  • Some setup needed. Running your own server takes a bit of tech comfort, especially for phone access over HTTPS.
  • No paid tier. The README says openGym is free and stays free, with nothing held back for sponsors. It accepts optional donations.
  • AI coach costs. The AI coach is off by default. If you turn it on, it uses your own key from a provider like Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini, which may charge for use. It can also use a free local option like Ollama.
  • Privacy: location data is removed from your own exercise photos before upload.
  • AGPL-3.0 license means you can use, change and share it for free. If you change it and run it as an online service for others, you must share your changed code under the same license.
  • Exercise animations have their own license. They belong to Gym visual and are licensed for use in openGym only.

The bottom line

openGym gives you a full-featured workout tracker with no subscription, no ads and no one else holding your data. It takes a little setup, but your training history is truly yours. Try the online demo first to see if it fits.
