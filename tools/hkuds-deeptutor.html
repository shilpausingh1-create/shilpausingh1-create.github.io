DeepTutor: a free AI tutor that learns from your own notes

DeepTutor is a free, open-source AI tutor that answers questions, makes quizzes, and builds study plans from your own notes, books, and videos.

StudentsEducation

stars: 41K · license: Apache-2.0 · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux (runs in your browser)

The problem it solves

You are stuck on a tricky chapter. It is late. There is no teacher to ask, and a private tutor costs a lot.

General AI chatbots can help, but they do not know your course. They have not read your textbook, your lecture slides, or your notes. So their answers can drift away from what you actually need to learn.

DeepTutor is built to be a study partner that works from your own materials.

What it is

DeepTutor is a free, open-source AI tutor from HKUDS, a lab at the University of Hong Kong. Open source means the code is public for anyone to use and check.

Its makers call it a learning workspace. In plain words, it is one place where you can chat with a tutor, take quizzes, research a topic, write notes, and track what you have learned.

You run it on your own computer, and you use it through your web browser. It does not come with its own AI brain. You connect an AI model provider, such as OpenAI, in its Settings.

Who it's for

  • High school and college students studying for exams.
  • Adult learners picking up a new skill on their own.
  • Teachers and tutors who want to build study materials from their lessons.
  • Parents, too. The multi-user mode has a Learner setting with guardian controls.
  • Anyone who learns from YouTube videos and wants a tutor beside the video.

What you can do with it

  • Chat with a tutor. Ask questions and get answers based on the files and notes you pick.
  • Make quizzes. Turn your materials into practice questions. A question bank keeps answers and explanations, and tracks your mistakes.
  • Get worked solutions. The Solve mode walks through a problem step by step.
  • Research a topic. The Research mode writes reports with sources.
  • Follow a study path. Mastery Path and Course Study guide you through a subject in order.
  • Learn from videos. Add a YouTube link and it plays the video, searches the transcript, and tutors you based on what was said at each moment.
  • Build a "living book." It turns your sources into an interactive book with quizzes, flash cards, timelines, and notes. It shows you the chapter outline first so you can approve it.
  • Write with help. Co-Writer lets you select a sentence and ask it to rewrite, expand, or shorten it. You can import and export Word files.
  • Track your tasks. A simple Task Board with To do, In progress, and Done columns.
  • See what it remembers. Its memory of you is visible and editable, not hidden.
  • Add skills. Download ready-made teaching skills, like a Socratic tutor or a flashcard builder, from its community hub, EduHub.
  • Use it in your language. Recent updates added French, German, Ukrainian, and Polish options.

How to start, step by step

The quickest trial uses Docker, a free app that runs other apps in a neat, sealed box. You will also need an account with an AI provider.

  1. Install Docker Desktop from docker.com and open it.
  2. Open Terminal (on Windows, use PowerShell) and paste this command:
docker run --rm --name deeptutor \
  -p 127.0.0.1:3782:3782 \
  -v deeptutor-data:/app/data \
  ghcr.io/hkuds/deeptutor:latest
  1. Open your web browser and go to http://127.0.0.1:3782.
  2. Go to Settings, then Providers, and add your AI account.
  3. Upload your notes or books, and start asking questions.
  4. When you are done, go back to Terminal and press Control and C to stop it. Your settings are saved for next time.

If you use Python, the README also offers an install with pip install -U deeptutor, followed by deeptutor init and deeptutor start.

Good to know

  • The app is free, the AI may not be. DeepTutor costs nothing, but the AI provider you connect may charge for use. Check your provider's prices.
  • Setup is a bit technical. You will use Terminal and Docker or Python. The README even offers a ready-made prompt you can give an AI coding helper to set it up for you.
  • Your files stay with you. It runs on your own computer, with your settings saved there. But the questions and materials you send to the AI go to that provider.
  • It can be shared. Turn on sign-in and one copy can serve a family, class, or team, each with their own private space. The first person to register becomes the admin.
  • Check its answers. Like any AI, it can make mistakes. The README even gives a checklist for reporting weak answers.
  • It changes fast. New versions come out often, so some screens may look different from screenshots.
  • License: Apache-2.0. You are free to use it, change it, and even use it in a business. Just keep the copyright and license notice.

The bottom line

DeepTutor gives you a patient AI tutor that studies from your own notes, books, and videos. It can quiz you, explain step by step, and guide you through a subject. It takes some setup and an AI account, but for serious learners it is a powerful free study tool.

Get it: github.com/HKUDS/DeepTutor
