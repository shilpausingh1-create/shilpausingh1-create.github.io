CorridorKey: clean green screen cutouts, even around hair

A free AI tool that pulls people cleanly off green or blue screens, keeping fine hair, motion blur and see-through edges.

CreatorsMediaMarketing

stars: 15K · license: Custom (CorridorKey Licence, non-commercial share-alike) · platforms: Windows, Mac (M1 or newer), Linux

The problem it solves

You film someone in front of a green screen. You remove the green. And then you see it: a fuzzy green outline around their hair. Choppy edges where their hand moved fast. A blurry shoulder that looks cut out with scissors.

This happens because edge pixels are a mix of the person and the green behind them. Old tools struggle to pull those colors apart. Many newer AI tools just make a hard on-or-off cutout, which throws away the soft, see-through details that make a shot look real.

Fixing it by hand can take hours. CorridorKey was built to do that work for you.

What it is

CorridorKey is a free AI "keyer." A keyer is the tool that removes a green or blue background from video.

It was made by Niko Pueringer, and the license and community are tied to the Corridor Digital team. You give it a green screen frame plus a rough mask of your subject. The AI then works out, pixel by pixel, the true color of your subject and how see-through each edge is. It rebuilds your subject as if the green screen was never there.

The rough mask does not need to be neat. The tool can even make one for you with optional add-ons.

Who it's for

  • YouTubers and video creators who shoot on green screen.
  • Small video teams and freelance editors.
  • Visual effects (VFX) artists who need clean edges for film or ads.
  • Marketing teams making product or spokesperson videos.
  • Students learning video effects.

Be aware: this is a new release run from a command window, not a polished app. If you want an easier, artist-friendly version, the README points to a separate project called EZ-CorridorKey.

What you can do with it

From the README:

  • Keep fine details: hair, motion blur and see-through areas stay soft and real.
  • Green or blue screen: it has separate AI models for each and can pick the right one by looking at your first frame.
  • Work in 4K: it scales to handle high-resolution footage.
  • Pro file formats: it reads and writes 16-bit and 32-bit EXR files (a high-quality image format used in film). These drop into tools like Nuke, Fusion or DaVinci Resolve.
  • Auto cleanup: it can remove tracking dots and tiny leftover specks in the background.
  • Despill control: it can remove the green color that bounces onto your subject, on a 0 to 10 scale.
  • Quick previews: it saves a ready-to-use preview you can drop into Premiere or Resolve, plus a checkerboard preview image.
  • Batch work: drop in one clip or a whole folder of shots at once.

How to start, step by step

What you need first: a computer with a decent graphics card. The README says the latest build should work with 6 to 8 GB of graphics memory (VRAM), and on most Macs with an M1 chip or newer. Windows users with NVIDIA cards need up-to-date drivers (CUDA 12.8 or higher), or it may fall back to the much slower main processor.

  1. Download the project from its GitHub page (the green "Code" button, then "Download ZIP"), and unzip it.
  2. Run the installer:
  3. Windows: double-click Install_CorridorKey_Windows.bat.
  4. Mac or Linux: open Terminal, type bash and a space, drag Install_CorridorKey_Linux_Mac.sh into the window, and press Enter.
  5. The installer sets up everything and downloads the AI model (about 300 MB) for you. You do not need to install Python yourself.
  6. Drag your video file or folder onto the launcher: CorridorKey_DRAG_CLIPS_HERE_local.bat on Windows, or CorridorKey_DRAG_CLIPS_HERE_local.sh on Mac and Linux.
  7. Answer the wizard's questions. It can sort your clips into folders and, if you like, make the rough mask for you.
  8. Pick "Process Ready Clips," choose your settings, and let it run.

Your results land in folders next to your clip, including a clean cutout and a quick preview.

Good to know

  • Optional mask helpers are heavy. Two add-ons, GVM and VideoMaMa, can make the rough mask for you. GVM needs about 80 GB of graphics memory, so most home computers cannot run it. You can make the rough mask in your own editing app instead.
  • Better mask, better result. The README says the quality of your rough mask matters a lot.
  • It is brand new. The maker says not everything has been tested and invites people to help fix bugs.
  • Cost: the tool is free. There is no paid service needed.
  • License, in plain words: you may use it for anything, including paid client work. But you may not repackage it and sell it, or sell it as a paid online service. If you share an improved version, it must use the same license and keep the CorridorKey name. Check the license file for full details. The optional add-ons have their own non-commercial licenses.

The bottom line

CorridorKey tackles the hardest part of green screen work: soft, messy edges. It is free, it runs on many home computers, and it keeps hair and motion blur looking natural. If you are comfortable following a short setup, it can save hours of cleanup.
