Career-Ops: a free AI job search helper that says when to skip

A free AI job search helper that checks each job against your CV, tells you when to skip, and drafts tailored CVs and cover letters.

Jobs & CareersEveryone

stars: 75K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux (inside an AI coding tool)

The problem it solves

Job hunting can feel like shouting into a void. You spend your evening rewriting a CV and cover letter. You hit send. Then nothing. No reply, ever.

Sometimes the job was a bad fit from the start. Sometimes it was already closed. Sometimes it was never real. Either way, you lost a night.

The creator of Career-Ops felt this too. He says he spent months sending CVs into silence, so he built the filter he needed. By his count, he looked at 740 listings, applied to 68, got 12 interviews and 1 offer. He took the job. Then he shared the tool for free.

What it is

Career-Ops is a free, open-source AI job search helper. You paste a job posting. It checks if the job is still open, compares it to your real CV, and tells you if it is worth your time.

If it is, it drafts a tailored CV, a cover letter and answers to the application questions. You read them. You decide. You press Submit.

The creator puts it simply: companies use AI to filter candidates, so he gave candidates AI to choose companies.

It runs on your own computer, inside an AI tool you chat with in a terminal, like Claude Code, Codex or Gemini's Antigravity CLI.

Who it's for

  • Job seekers in any field who are tired of applying blindly.
  • People changing careers who want to know which roles fit.
  • Recent grads who need help tailoring a CV for each role.
  • Anyone preparing for interviews or weighing a job offer.

You do need to be okay with a little setup in a terminal window. After that, you mostly just chat with it. The built-in company list leans toward tech and AI companies, but you can add your own.

What you can do with it

From the README:

  • Check the job is live. It confirms the posting is still open before you write a word, and can flag likely reposts.
  • Score the fit. It compares the role to your CV and tells you to skip weak matches. It will advise against applying below 4.0 out of 5. You can override it.
  • Tailored CVs. It makes a CV for each job, built from your own experience, in a format that job sites' screening software (ATS) can read.
  • Cover letters. It drafts research-backed cover letters and turns them into PDFs.
  • Find the right person. It identifies a hiring manager or recruiter and drafts a short LinkedIn note. It never sends it.
  • Company research. It digs into a company's recent moves and culture.
  • Interview prep. Practice questions with feedback, prep plans, and a bank of stories to reuse.
  • Offer help. It walks through a contract with you and lists questions for a lawyer, plus salary negotiation scripts.
  • Learn from no's. After a run of rejections, it names the skill gap.
  • Track everything. A dashboard shows all your applications in one place.

How to start, step by step

What you need first: Node.js (a free program that runs the installer) and an AI terminal tool like Claude Code, Codex or Antigravity CLI. If you already use one of those, you likely have Node.js.

  1. Open Terminal and paste:
npx @santifer/career-ops init
  1. This creates a new career-ops folder. Move into it by typing cd career-ops.
  2. Open your AI tool there. For Claude Code, type claude.
  3. On first launch, it asks for your CV, what you want and what you refuse, just by chatting. Nothing to edit by hand.
  4. Paste a job link or description. It runs the full check and tells you what to do next.

The creator warns that the first runs are rough because it does not know you yet. Think of it as a recruiter's first week.

Good to know

  • It never applies for you. It drafts and fills, but you always press Submit. It also never sends emails.
  • Your data stays with you. There is no tracking. Your CV goes only from your computer to the AI provider you choose.
  • AI costs: the tool is free. Your AI provider may charge you. The README says it can also run on free and local models, and has a guide for running on a budget.
  • Read every CV before sending. It is told to reword, never invent. A built-in check blocks made-up numbers or facts, but it cannot judge every rewording yet.
  • Follow job site rules. Use it within the terms of the job sites you visit, and do not spam employers.
  • License (MIT): free to use, change and even use in a business. Just keep the notice. The "career-ops" name itself has a separate trademark policy.

The bottom line

Career-Ops helps you apply to fewer jobs, but better ones. It tells you when to skip, does the heavy writing when a job fits, and leaves the final call to you. If job hunting is eating your evenings, it is worth a try.
