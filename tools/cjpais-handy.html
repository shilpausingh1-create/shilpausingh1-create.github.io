Handy: talk instead of type, free and fully offline

Handy is a free, open-source app that turns your voice into text in any app, all processed privately on your own computer.

EveryoneStudentsJobs & Careers

stars: 34K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux

The problem it solves

Some days it feels like all you do is type. Emails. Messages. Notes. Reports. Your thoughts move fast, but your fingers cannot keep up.

Voice typing tools exist, but many have a catch. Some cost a monthly fee. Others send your voice to a company's servers to be turned into text. That is not great if you are dictating private notes, work emails, or anything personal.

Handy gives you voice typing that is free and stays on your own computer.

What it is

Handy is a free, open-source speech-to-text app. Open source means its code is public for anyone to read and improve. Speech-to-text simply means it turns what you say into written words.

The idea is simple. Press a keyboard shortcut. Speak. Your words appear in whatever text box you are using. It could be an email, a chat, a document, or a search bar.

Everything happens on your own computer. The README says nothing is sent to the cloud. Once it is set up, it works fully offline.

Its makers sum up the goal in four words: free, open source, private, and simple. One tool, one job.

Who it's for

  • Writers and bloggers who think faster than they type.
  • Students taking notes or drafting essays.
  • Job seekers writing cover letters and emails.
  • Busy professionals answering lots of messages.
  • People who find typing hard or tiring. The makers say accessibility tools belong in everyone's hands.
  • Anyone who wants private voice typing without a subscription.

What you can do with it

  • Type with your voice anywhere. Your words are pasted right into the app you are using.
  • Choose how the shortcut works. Hold the key to record and let go to stop. Or tap once to start and tap again to stop.
  • Stay private. Your voice stays on your computer. No audio goes to the cloud.
  • Work offline. No internet needed once your model is downloaded.
  • Pick your AI model. A model is the trained "brain" that turns speech into text. Handy offers Whisper models (Small, Medium, Turbo, Large) that use your graphics chip when available. It also offers Parakeet V3, which runs well on a regular processor and detects your language automatically.
  • Skip the silence. It filters out quiet parts so only your speech gets processed.
  • Use it on any computer. It works on Mac, Windows, and Linux.
  • Control it from Raycast. A community add-on lets Mac users of the Raycast launcher start and stop recording and browse past transcripts.

How to start, step by step

You need a Mac, Windows, or Linux computer with a microphone.

  1. Go to the Handy Releases page on GitHub, or the website handy.computer, and download the version for your computer.
  2. Install the app like any other program.
  3. Open Handy. It will ask for two permissions. Allow microphone access (so it can hear you) and accessibility access (so it can paste text into other apps).
  4. Open Settings and choose your keyboard shortcut.
  5. Click into any text box, press your shortcut, and start talking. Release, and your words appear.

Mac users with Homebrew (a free app installer) can also install it with one line:

brew install --cask handy

On Windows, you can use winget install cjpais.Handy. Note that the README says these two install options are not run by the Handy team.

Good to know

  • It is free. No subscription and no paid tier mentioned in the README.
  • Privacy is the point. Your voice is processed on your computer, not sent online.
  • First-time download. Handy needs to download a speech model before it works. Sizes range from about 487 MB to 1.6 GB. If you are behind a strict work firewall, the README explains how to add models by hand.
  • Bluetooth headsets on Mac. Using a Bluetooth headset mic can make your audio sound worse while recording. The README suggests picking your Mac's built-in mic in Handy instead.
  • The fn key only works on Apple keyboards. If you use another brand of keyboard, choose a shortcut with keys like Control, Option, Shift, or Command.
  • Linux takes more setup. Linux users may need to install an extra helper tool for typing to work, especially on newer systems.
  • It is still growing. The makers say it is actively being developed and has some known issues.
  • License: MIT. You are free to use it, change it, and even use it in a business. Just keep the copyright notice. The Handy name and logo are not open source, so copies must use their own branding.

The bottom line

Handy lets you talk instead of type in any app, for free. Your voice never leaves your computer, and it works offline. If you write a lot, or typing is a chore, it is an easy tool to try today.

Get it: github.com/cjpais/Handy
