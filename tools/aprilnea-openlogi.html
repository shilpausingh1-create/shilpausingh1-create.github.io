OpenLogi: a light, free replacement for Logitech Options+

OpenLogi is a free, lightweight app that controls Logitech mice, keyboards, webcams and lights without Logitech's own heavy software.

EveryoneCreatorsDevelopers

stars: 24K · license: Apache-2.0 · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux

The problem it solves

You bought a nice Logitech mouse. Maybe an MX Master with all those extra buttons. To set them up, you install Logitech Options+.

For many people, that is where the trouble starts. The software can feel heavy for what it does. And if you use Linux, there is no official version at all.

OpenLogi offers another way. It is a small, free app that unlocks the same hardware, runs on your own computer, and works on Mac, Windows and Linux.

What it is

OpenLogi is a free, open-source alternative to Logitech Options+. Open source means anyone can read the code that runs it.

The README calls it "native" and "local-first." Native means it is built for your computer directly, so it stays light and quick. Local-first means it works on your machine, not through an online service. It is written in Rust, a programming language known for speed and safety.

It talks to Logitech mice, keyboards, webcams and Litra lights. You can remap buttons, set up gestures, tune scrolling, and adjust your webcam picture, all from one place.

All your settings live in one plain text file. You can copy it to another computer however you like.

OpenLogi is not made by Logitech. It is an independent project.

Who it's for

  • Logitech mouse owners who want their extra buttons to do more.
  • Linux users, who get no official Logitech app.
  • Video callers and streamers who want better control of a Logitech webcam.
  • Designers, editors and coders who want different button setups for different apps.
  • People who want a lighter app running in the background.

What you can do with it

From the README:

  • See your devices connected by Logi Bolt or Unifying receiver, Bluetooth or cable, with battery level and charging state.
  • Remap buttons, including custom keyboard shortcuts. Set different actions for a short press and a long press, or hold a button for push-to-talk.
  • Per-app profiles. Button setups switch on their own when you change apps. (On Mac and Windows; on Linux only with certain setups.)
  • Gestures. Hold a supported button and move the mouse in a direction to trigger an action. Or turn gestures off.
  • Actions Ring. A circle of eight shortcuts that pops up around your cursor.
  • DPI control, which sets how fast your pointer moves, with presets.
  • SmartShift wheel settings, for the scroll wheel that switches between clicky and free-spinning.
  • Keyboards: remap F-keys to actions, typed text or multi-step shortcuts, plus basic RGB lighting on supported models.
  • Webcams: works with Logitech webcams like Brio, StreamCam and the C920 series. Adjust zoom, focus, exposure, brightness, white balance and more. The changes are saved to the camera, so they also apply in Zoom, Meet and OBS.
  • Webcam presets: Default, Streaming and Video call, plus your own.
  • Privacy-friendly preview: the camera only turns on while you watch the preview. Leave it and the light goes off.
  • Litra lights: power, brightness and color warmth, with an option to turn on when your camera does.

How to start, step by step

What you need first: a Logitech device. On a Mac, macOS 13 or newer. Windows and Linux are supported too.

  1. Quit Logi Options+ first. The README says both apps fight over the same connection, and only one can control a receiver at a time.
  2. Download OpenLogi:
  3. Mac: get the .dmg file from the Releases page on GitHub and drag OpenLogi into Applications. Or, if you use Homebrew (a free installer for Mac apps), run the command below.
  4. Windows: get the .msi installer or the portable .zip from the Releases page.
  5. Linux: the README gives a safe install script that checks the download before installing.
  6. Open OpenLogi.
  7. Pick your device and start setting up buttons, scrolling or your webcam.

The Homebrew command for Mac:

brew install --cask openlogi

If you use the portable Windows .zip, keep the two program files side by side. One is the window you see, the other is a background helper that talks to your devices.

Good to know

  • It is still in active development. The README warns it is not yet stable, and features and settings may change.
  • Windows support is newer than the Mac version. It has been tested on Windows 11 with real hardware, but you may hit rough edges.
  • Not every feature works on every device. Several features only work on models that support them.
  • Some Linux limits. A few Mac-only actions do nothing on Linux, and per-app profiles need X11 or XWayland (types of Linux display systems).
  • License, in plain words. The code is free under your choice of the Apache 2.0 or MIT license. You can use it, change it and share it. The OpenLogi name and logo are not covered, so forks must use their own.
  • Not affiliated with Logitech. Logitech, MX Master and Options+ are Logitech trademarks.

The bottom line

OpenLogi lets your Logitech gear do everything it can, without the heavy official app. It is free, light and works on Linux too. Just remember it is still young, so expect a few changes along the way.
