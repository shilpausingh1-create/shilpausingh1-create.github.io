Open Notebook: a private NotebookLM you fully own

A free, private alternative to Google NotebookLM that chats with your files and turns notes into podcasts.

StudentsEducationBusiness

stars: 40K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux (runs in your browser via Docker)

The problem it solves

AI research notebooks are great. You drop in PDFs and web pages, then ask questions and get answers with sources. Google NotebookLM made this popular.

But there is a catch. Your files go to Google's servers, and you can only use Google's AI models. If your notes are private, like client files, medical research, legal papers or unpublished work, that can be a real problem.

What it is

Open Notebook is a free, open-source alternative to Google NotebookLM. You run it yourself, on your own computer or server, and use it in your web browser.

You bring the AI. It works with more than 20 AI providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral and others. Or you can run free AI models on your own machine with Ollama (a free app that runs AI on your computer). With local models, nothing leaves your machine.

Who it's for

  • Students who want to study from lecture PDFs, videos and articles in one place.
  • Researchers and academics who need private notes and answers with citations.
  • Lawyers, consultants and analysts who work with confidential documents.
  • Healthcare and policy workers who want to keep sensitive reading off big-tech servers.
  • Writers and podcasters who want to turn research into audio shows.
  • Teachers who want to build study material from their own sources.

What you can do with it

  • Add many kinds of sources. PDFs, videos, audio, web pages, Office documents and more.
  • Chat with your research. Ask questions and get answers that cite the sources and notes they used.
  • Make podcasts from your notes. Create shows with 1 to 4 speakers and custom speaker profiles. NotebookLM offers only 2.
  • Search everything. Find things by exact words or by meaning across all your content.
  • Organize by project. Keep separate notebooks for separate topics.
  • Write notes with AI help, or write them yourself.
  • Choose what the AI sees. Pick exactly which material is shared with the AI model.
  • Use it in your language. The interface comes in English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Chinese, Portuguese and several others.
  • Add a password if you put it on a shared network or online.

How to start, step by step

First you need: Docker Desktop (a free app that runs other apps in sealed boxes) for Mac or Windows, or Docker Engine on Linux. You also need either an AI provider key (a secret password for an AI service) or a free local model with Ollama.

  1. Open Terminal and download the setup file:
mkdir open-notebook && cd open-notebook
curl -o docker-compose.yml https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lfnovo/open-notebook/main/docker-compose.yml
  1. Open docker-compose.yml in a text editor. Find the line that says change-me-to-a-secret-string and replace it with your own long random secret. This protects the AI keys you save. Keep it safe.
  2. Start it:
docker compose up -d
  1. Wait about 30 seconds, then open http://localhost:8502 in your browser.
  2. In the sidebar, open Models, pick your AI provider, click Add Configuration, and paste your key (Ollama needs a web address instead). Click Test to check it.
  3. Add at least one language model and one embedding model (the part that powers search), then click Auto-assign Defaults.
  4. Create a notebook, add a source, and start chatting.

Good to know

  • The app is free. AI use may not be. You pay your chosen AI provider for what you use. Running local models with Ollama avoids those costs.
  • Some providers need a partner. Anthropic and Groq do not offer embedding models, so pair them with a provider that does, like OpenAI or Google.
  • Security on shared networks. By default there is no password, and it can be reached from other devices on your network. The README explains how to limit it to your computer or add a password before starting.
  • Privacy depends on your choice. With a cloud AI provider, the text you send goes to that provider. For full privacy, use local models.
  • Needs some setup. It is not a one-click app. There is an AI install helper and a troubleshooting guide linked in the README.
  • License. It uses MIT. In plain words: free to use, change and share, even in paid work, as long as you keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

Open Notebook gives you the best parts of NotebookLM without handing your research to Google. You choose the AI, you keep the files, and you can even make multi-speaker podcasts. If your notes are private, it is well worth the short setup.

Get it: github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook
