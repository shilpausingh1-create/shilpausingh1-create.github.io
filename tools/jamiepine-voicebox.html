Voicebox: a free, private AI voice studio on your own computer

A free app that clones voices, reads text aloud in 23 languages, and lets you dictate into any app, all on your own computer.

CreatorsMediaEducation

stars: 57K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux

The problem it solves

AI voices are everywhere now. Creators use them for voiceovers. Teachers use them for lessons. Small businesses use them for ads and phone menus. But the popular tools charge every month, and your voice recordings get uploaded to someone else's servers.

Typing is another pain. Dictation apps that turn speech into text often cost money too, and they also send your words to the cloud.

What it is

Voicebox is a free, open-source "AI voice studio" that runs on your own computer. It calls itself a free alternative to ElevenLabs (a paid AI voice service) and WisprFlow (a paid dictation app), in one app.

It does both halves of voice work. It can turn text into speech, including speech in a cloned voice. And it can turn your speech into text, so you can dictate into any app. The README says models, voice data and recordings never leave your machine.

Who it's for

  • Content creators and YouTubers who need voiceovers without a monthly bill.
  • Podcasters who want to build multi-voice episodes or fix lines.
  • Teachers and course makers who want lessons read aloud in many languages.
  • Writers and busy professionals who would rather talk than type.
  • Game and story makers who want character voices.
  • People who have lost their voice, since the README lists speech help as a use case.
  • Developers who want to give apps or AI helpers a voice.

What you can do with it

  • Clone a voice from a few seconds of audio, or pick from 50+ ready-made preset voices.
  • Speak 23 languages, from English to Arabic, Japanese, Hindi and Swahili.
  • Choose from 8 voice engines, each with different strengths, and switch per clip.
  • Describe a voice in words and have one built for you (with the Qwen VoiceDesign engine).
  • Add emotion. With the Chatterbox Turbo engine, tags like [laugh], [sigh] or [whispering] change how lines sound.
  • Add effects like pitch shift, reverb, echo and filters, with presets such as Radio and Deep Voice.
  • Make long audio. It splits long text into chunks and blends them, up to 50,000 characters.
  • Edit stories. A timeline editor lets you arrange several voices for podcasts and conversations.
  • Dictate anywhere. Hold a hotkey, speak, and let go. On a Mac, the text pastes into the box you are typing in.
  • Clean up your speech. An optional built-in AI can remove "ums" and false starts.
  • Give AI helpers a voice. Tools like Claude Code or Cursor can speak to you in a voice you choose.

How to start, step by step

You need a Mac or a Windows computer. A good graphics chip helps, but the README says it also works on a regular processor, just slower.

  1. Download the app. From the README: Mac with Apple chip (DMG), Mac with Intel (DMG), or Windows (MSI). Links are at voicebox.sh.
  2. Open Voicebox. Create a voice profile by recording yourself in the app or adding an audio file. Or pick a preset voice.
  3. Type your text and generate speech. Then add effects or try another take.
  4. Try dictation. Set your hotkey, hold it, speak, and release. On a Mac, the app walks you through the permissions it needs.

Linux users: there are no ready-made downloads yet. The README points to voicebox.sh/linux-install for build-it-yourself steps. Docker users can run docker compose up.

Good to know

  • Free, no ads. The README says there are no splash screens, banners or interruptions.
  • Private by design. Voices, models and recordings stay on your computer.
  • Model downloads. The voice engines are downloaded to your computer, so expect to use some disk space.
  • Auto-paste is Mac only for now. Windows and Linux auto-paste for dictation is on the roadmap.
  • Use voices fairly. Only clone voices you have the right to use, like your own or with clear permission.
  • License: MIT. Free to use, change and even use in business. Just keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

Voicebox puts a voice studio and a dictation tool in one free app that keeps your audio at home. If you pay monthly for AI voices or dictation, it is worth a try. Download it, record a few seconds, and hear the result.
