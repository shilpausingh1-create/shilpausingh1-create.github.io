Compositor: a free Photoshop-style photo editor for your Mac

A free, open-source Mac image editor with layers, masks, blend modes and Photoshop-style shortcuts, and no monthly subscription.

DesignCreatorsMarketing

stars: 17K · license: MIT · platforms: Mac

The problem it solves

Photoshop is great. But you pay for it every month, forever. For a hobby photographer, a small business owner or a student, that adds up fast.

Free editors exist, but many feel strange if you learned on Photoshop. The menus are different. The shortcuts are different. You lose your flow.

Compositor's creator felt the same way. In the README, he says Photoshop costs too much and tools like GIMP didn't feel familiar enough. So he built his own.

What it is

Compositor is a free, open-source image editor for Mac. It's built around the Photoshop way of working: layers, masks, blend modes and adjustments.

It's made for compositing (combining several images into one) and for polishing photos until they look just right.

Because it's open source, anyone can download the code and add, remove or change features.

Who it's for

  • Photographers who want to edit photos with familiar tools, without a subscription.
  • Graphic designers who build posters, thumbnails and social posts from layers.
  • Small business owners making product photos or flyers.
  • Students learning image editing on a budget.
  • Marketers and creators who need quick, clean visuals.
  • Ex-Photoshop users who already know the shortcuts.

What you can do with it

Layers - Layers and folders, with opacity and Photoshop's full set of blend modes. - Layer masks you can paint, fill, blur and feather. Clipping masks too. - Adjustment layers like Curves, Levels, Hue/Saturation, Exposure, Black & White, Color Balance and Gaussian Blur. - Layer effects like Stroke, Drop Shadow, Outer Glow and Color Overlay, editable any time.

Moving things around - Non-destructive move, scale, rotate and flip. "Non-destructive" means you can shrink an image and grow it back with no loss in quality. - Free distort, snapping to edges and guides, and exact number inputs.

Selecting - Marquee, Lasso and a Magic tool that selects by color or traces an object you click. - Select Subject, and Content-Aware Fill, which can also extend a photo past its edges.

Painting and fixing - Brush, Spot Healing Brush, Clone Stamp and Blur tool. - Gradient and Shape tools that stay editable. - A Type tool for text.

Filters - A Camera Raw-style panel for light, color, curves and detail. - Noise, Vignette, Bloom, Lens Correction and Remove Background, with live previews.

Files - Opens JPEG, PNG, HEIC, TIFF, WebP, SVG, camera RAW, and Photoshop PSD and PSB files (8-bit RGB, not CMYK). Many Photoshop folders, masks and blend modes stay editable. - Exports PNG, JPEG or a one-page PDF. - Photoshop-style keyboard shortcuts you can change. - Tabs for many projects, and a command search (⌘F) to find any tool by name.

Works with AI - AI helpers and scripts can build and edit Compositor projects directly. An open project updates live as they work.

How to start, step by step

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (an M-series chip) running macOS 26 or later.

  1. Download it. Get the latest version from the GitHub Releases page or from robbietilton.com/compositor.
  2. Install it like any Mac app: open the download and drag Compositor into your Applications folder.
  3. Open Compositor and drop in a photo to start editing.

Prefer Homebrew (a free tool for installing Mac apps from Terminal)? Run:

brew install --cask robbietilton-compositor

The app updates itself automatically, and the README says builds are signed and notarized (checked by Apple).

Good to know

  • Mac only. It needs Apple silicon and macOS 26.0 or later. Older Intel Macs are not supported.
  • English only for now. The creator says translations aren't being accepted yet, because one person maintains the app.
  • Photoshop files have limits. CMYK files (a print color format) are not supported. Some vector shapes and vertical text become flat pixels. Compositor shows a report before it changes anything.
  • It's made by one person. That's impressive, but it also means updates depend on one maintainer.
  • No cost. There's no subscription or paid tier mentioned in the README.
  • MIT license means you can use, change and share it for free, even for paid client work. Just keep the copyright notice.

The bottom line

Compositor gives Mac users a familiar, Photoshop-style editor for free. It has layers, masks, real adjustments and the shortcuts you already know. If you're tired of paying every month, it's well worth a download.

Get it: github.com/robbietilton/Compositor
