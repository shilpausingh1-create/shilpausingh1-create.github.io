OpenMontage: turn your AI helper into a free video studio

Describe a video in plain words and your AI assistant researches, scripts, narrates, edits and renders it for you.

CreatorsMarketingEducation

stars: 68K · license: AGPL-3.0 · platforms: Mac, Windows, Linux (with an AI coding assistant)

The problem it solves

Making one good video takes a lot of jobs. Someone has to research the topic. Someone writes the script. Someone records a voice, finds pictures or clips, picks music, adds captions, and edits it all together. For a small creator or a busy teacher, that can eat days for a single minute of video.

Most AI video tools do not fix this. They give you one short clip from a prompt, and you still do the rest by hand.

What it is

OpenMontage is a free, open-source video production system. It turns your AI coding assistant (a chat helper that can read files and run small programs on your computer, like Claude Code or Cursor) into a full video team.

You describe the video you want in plain words. The AI then follows a set step by step plan: research, script, scene plan, gathering pictures and clips, editing, and putting the final video together. It stops to ask for your approval at the creative choices.

It can make videos from AI images. It can also build a real "footage" video by pulling free clips from open sources like Archive.org, NASA and Wikimedia Commons, then cutting them together.

Who it's for

  • YouTubers and short-form creators who want explainers or montages without days of editing.
  • Teachers and trainers who want a quick animated lesson, like "why the sky is blue."
  • Marketers and small business owners who need a product teaser or launch clip.
  • Podcasters who want to turn long episodes into short social clips.

You do need to be comfortable pasting a few commands into a terminal (the text window where you type commands to your computer). After setup, you mostly talk to your AI in plain English.

What you can do with it

  • Start from a video you love. Paste a YouTube video, Short, Reel, TikTok or local clip. The AI studies its pacing and style, then offers 2 to 3 original ideas for your own topic.
  • See the cost first. Before it makes anything, it gives a cost estimate for your video length. It also supports spend caps.
  • Watch it work live. A local board called Backlot shows each stage as it runs: the script, scene cards, and money spent. You approve the visuals scene by scene before the final render.
  • Make videos with zero paid keys. Out of the box it includes free narration (Piper, a free offline voice), free archive footage, music finding, word-by-word captions, and editing tools.
  • Pick a type of video. It has more than 10 ready-made workflows, including animated explainer, documentary montage, talking head, screen demo, podcast repurpose, cinematic trailer, and translate-and-dub.
  • Real research built in. Before writing the script, the AI searches the web and gathers facts, so the video is grounded in real information.
  • Use better tools if you have them. Add optional accounts for AI video, image, music or voice services to unlock fancier results.

How to start, step by step

First you need: Python 3.10 or newer, FFmpeg (a free video tool), Node.js 18 or newer, and an AI coding assistant such as Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, Windsurf or Codex.

  1. Open Terminal and paste these three lines:
git clone https://github.com/calesthio/OpenMontage.git
cd OpenMontage
make setup
  1. Open the new OpenMontage folder in your AI coding assistant.
  2. Ask for a video in plain words, for example: "Make a 60-second animated explainer about how neural networks learn."
  3. For the free real-footage path, try something like: "Make a 75-second documentary montage about city life in the rain. Use real footage only, no narration, with music."
  4. Answer the AI when it asks you to approve the plan or visuals. The finished video lands in your project folder.

No "make" on your computer? The README lists other setup commands, including one for Windows.

Good to know

  • Free path vs paid path. The free setup makes real videos using free voices, open footage and AI-made layouts. Fancier AI-generated video clips need paid services. The README's example prompts with an image or video provider run about $0.15 to $1.50, and a "full setup" trailer about $1 to $3. One sample short film cost $1.33.
  • Your AI helper may cost money. Tools like Claude Code or Cursor often need a paid plan of their own.
  • Free stock keys. Pexels, Pixabay and Unsplash keys are optional and free to get.
  • Have a gaming graphics card? You can turn on free local AI video generation on your own machine.
  • License. It uses AGPL-3.0. In plain words: free to use and change, but if you share a changed version, or run a changed version as an online service for others, you must share your source code too.
  • No click-and-drag window. Your AI assistant is the "director," following instruction files in the project.

The bottom line

OpenMontage gives anyone with an AI coding helper a full video crew: researcher, writer, narrator and editor. It can make real videos for free, and it tells you the price before spending anything. If you make explainers, lessons or promos, it can turn days of work into a conversation.
